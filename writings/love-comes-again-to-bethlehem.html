THE GREATEST LOVE STORY EVER TOLD · WORKING DRAFT

Love Comes Again to Bethlehem

The years carried the family onward. Children grew into parents, familiar names became memories, and generations followed roads their ancestors would never see. From this distance, kingdoms seem to rise and fall around them like passing weather. Yet within those years were people living each day, loving one another, losing one another, and wondering whether the promises they had inherited would ever come within reach.

There were voices along the way. Through the prophets, the people heard that God had not forgotten them. Into their wandering and captivity came promises of deliverance, of gathering, of coming home. Isaiah spoke of good news for the poor, of broken hearts bound up, and liberty proclaimed to captives. Those words remained as the people who first heard them grew old. Others received them, read them aloud, and carried them into years still waiting to unfold.

Before we follow those years any further, perhaps we should turn our imagined scroll over again. We have watched the human side, where a lifetime can pass beneath our eyes in a sentence. We have crossed griefs that those who endured them could scarcely cross in years. From the other side, what might it mean to know and love every person whose name we have passed?

We can approach that question only a little way. Our compassion grows weary; sometimes we must turn away from what we cannot bear. But the love we are following continues toward those who suffer. Beneath every promise of deliverance is a compassion deeper than we can measure. As we glimpse something of it, perhaps we begin to understand why we find ourselves saying that He had to come.

When we turn back, the road is leading once more toward Bethlehem.

We have been here before. Naomi returned along this road carrying a sorrow she could not repair, with Ruth beside her. There were fields where grain was gathered, a meal where a stranger was welcomed, and a redeemer who attended to what he had promised. In time, Naomi held Obed in her arms. Beyond that small circle of lives lay Jesse, David, and generations none of them would meet.

Now Joseph comes to Bethlehem with Mary, who is expecting a child. He belongs to David’s house, and the journey brings that family line back into view. We draw closer again, as we have before, to people whose immediate concerns are the road, a place to stay, and a birth drawing near. While they are there, Mary gives birth to her son, wraps Him, and lays Him in a manger.

For a moment, we can remain with this young family. A child needs warmth and care. His mother holds Him, and Joseph is there beside them. The story that has carried us across the generations has brought us close enough to see another baby in Bethlehem. His name is Jesus.

Outside the town, shepherds are watching their flocks through the night. An angel comes to them, and the glory of the Lord shines around them. They are afraid, but the message is one of joy: in David’s town a Savior has been born, Christ the Lord. They will find Him wrapped and lying in a manger.

Then the night fills with a multitude of the heavenly host praising God. From the fields outside Bethlehem, we hear glory given to God in the highest, and peace proclaimed on earth. The long years of promise have brought us to this night, and heaven is rejoicing over the child.

The shepherds go to see what they have been told. They find Mary and Joseph, and the baby lying in the manger. They tell what they have heard, while Mary keeps these things and ponders them in her heart. We need not hurry beyond her. We have followed the family through so much time; now we can stay a little while in Bethlehem, where love has come again, and the angels’ praise still accompanies the story.
