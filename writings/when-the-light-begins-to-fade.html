When the Light Begins to Fade

I’ve always thought of going to bed as the end of my day. I finish what I can, go to sleep, and figure the next day will start when I get up. But Genesis puts evening first. “And the evening and the morning were the first day” (Genesis 1:5). In the Jewish reckoning, a new day begins in the evening. I’ve been thinking about what that might mean for the way I live with God.

As the sun goes down, I can look back over the hours of light and thank Him for what He did. Sometimes I remember something I was too busy to notice when it happened. I have time now to think about it and say, Father, thank You. I see a little more of what You were doing there.

Then I can give myself to Him for the day that is beginning. I haven’t put my feet on the floor to go anywhere yet. I haven’t decided what I can get done. I can lie down and ask Him to renew my body, soul, and spirit while I rest. I can ask Him to renew my mind so I can listen more closely to the Holy Spirit.

When the light comes again, I have time to act on what He has brought to mind. It may be a person I need to call or something I need to do with more patience than I gave it yesterday. I want to be attentive enough to follow through. That may be the very place where someone sees a little of the Father in me.

I think that’s what I mean when I ask Him to refresh His image in me. I want the time I spend with Him in the quiet to reach the next person I meet in the light.

And when the light begins to fade again, I have something more to thank Him for.

Father, thank You for what You did in the light today. I give You the day that is beginning. Renew me as I rest, and let someone see You in me when the light returns.
