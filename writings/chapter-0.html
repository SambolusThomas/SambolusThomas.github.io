THE GREATEST LOVE STORY EVER TOLD · WORKING DRAFT

From Separation to Reconciliation

The Greatest Love Story Ever Told

An Opening

There is a story so extraordinary that, if it is true, it changes everything. It has been told for thousands of years, translated into hundreds of languages, argued over, fought over, defended, dismissed, and sometimes even forbidden. Millions know something about it. 

Far fewer have stopped to consider what it is actually saying. Perhaps that is because the story makes an almost unbelievable claim.

That the Creator of everything—of every star, every galaxy, every living thing, and even the passage of time itself—cares about us.

Not merely about humanity as a whole.

About you.

About me.

Personally.

Not only does He care, but He has been pursuing a relationship with us since before we knew there was a relationship to be had.

I cannot tell you that I understand everything about such a Being. I cannot explain the boundaries of One who, by definition, has none. I certainly cannot claim to know all His purposes, but I can tell you something from my own life.

I believe God loves me.

And I believe He has pursued me.

Not because I deserved His attention. Not because I was particularly wise or faithful. And certainly not because I have always listened.

There have been times when I was too occupied with my own affairs to notice. Times when I thought I knew better. Times when I wandered far from the relationship I now treasure.

Yet, looking back, I see a patience and a love I cannot adequately describe.

And that has made me wonder about something much larger than my own experience.

What if the same love has been pursuing humanity from the very beginning?

What if the ancient writings we call the Bible are not merely a record of people trying to find God, but the unfolding account of God coming to find us?

Consider what we have in our hands.

Sixty-six books, written across many centuries, by people living in different lands, speaking different languages, facing different circumstances. Kings and shepherds, prophets and fishermen, historians and poets.

Some of these writers lived long before others were born.

Yet Christians have recognized in their writings a remarkable story that unfolds from creation to the promise of a new heaven and a new earth.

A story of beginnings and endings.

Of promises and betrayals.

Of judgment and mercy.

Of separation and reconciliation.

And through it all, a recurring question:

Who is the One at the center of this story?

Suppose we approach these writings as an investigator might approach an ancient mystery.

We do not have to pretend every question has been answered. We do not have to overlook difficulties or manufacture connections where none exist.

We can examine the testimony, follow the recurring themes, consider the history, and ask whether the pieces belong together.

But there is something unusual about this investigation.

As we follow the story, we may discover that we are not standing entirely outside it.

We may be part of the very thing we are investigating.

The Scroll

Imagine an ancient scroll written on both sides.

On one side is the familiar human story.

We see our beginnings, our desires, our accomplishments, our terrible failures, our wars, our suffering, and our persistent attempts to build a world in which we can be happy.

It is a complicated story, sometimes magnificent and sometimes heartbreaking.

Now turn the scroll over.

There is writing on the other side.

Another account has been unfolding all along.

Not a competing story, nor an explanation hastily added after humanity went wrong.

Something older.

A purpose that reaches back before the foundation of the world.

The two sides are inseparable. They belong to the same scroll, though those living on one side cannot see the whole of what is written on the other.

And what holds them together?

That is the mystery we are going to explore.

For now, consider one possibility.

What if the story we know best—our own—is only one side of the greatest love story ever told?

And what if the One who knows both sides has already told us how it ends?
