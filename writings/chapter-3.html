CHAPTER 3 · WORKING DRAFT

Chapter Three

When Terah gathered his household for the journey, he gathered people whose lives were bound together. Abram was his son. Sarai was his daughter-in-law. Lot was his grandson, the son of a man they had lost.

Whatever hopes or disappointments traveled with them, they traveled as a family.

That is worth remembering, because family had belonged to the human story before there was anything to hide, before shame entered the garden, and before the first man and woman turned away from their Creator. They had been given to one another while the world was still good.

What happened afterward wounded those relationships. We have already seen affection give way to accusation. Yet the bonds themselves remained. People continued to marry, bear children, care for their households, and grieve when someone was missing.

We might imagine Terah looking over those gathered for the road, counting belongings and considering the needs of each person. There was a place they intended to go. There were people he expected to bring safely there.

How much he understood of what lay ahead, we cannot know.

In Haran, his journey ended. The household he had brought there would continue beyond him.

A Promise Within the Household

Abram had heard the call of God, but he did not travel through life alone. Sarai shared the uncertainty of the road and the unfamiliarity of each new place. The future spoken of by God would reach into her life as surely as his.

And there was something neither of them could overlook.

They had no child.

God spoke of descendants. Abram and Sarai lived with the absence of one.

Consider how such a promise might be heard within a household. There were possessions to manage, work to accomplish, and decisions to make each day. But beyond all those ordinary responsibilities lay a question they could not settle by attending more carefully to their affairs.

How would this promised future come?

As the years unfolded, God's commitment to Abram would be expressed in a word we need to carry with us.

Covenant.

We will take time to understand it. For now, notice that God was committing Himself to a relationship extending beyond the remaining years of this one man's life. He spoke of being God to Abraham and to his descendants after him.

There would be responsibilities within that relationship. Abraham was called to walk before God, and his household would bear the sign of the covenant. But its future rested upon the faithfulness of the One who established it.

Abraham could respond. He could obey. He could also misunderstand, become afraid, and attempt to secure an outcome he could not yet see.

He would have much to learn about trusting God.

So would his family.

The Generations Ahead

In time, we will meet Isaac, the son Abraham and Sarah had waited for. Then Jacob, and the household that would grow around him.

Let us allow them to arrive as people.

Each will have a life to live, relationships to navigate, and choices whose consequences reach others. The family will know tenderness and rivalry, faith and fear, departures and reunions. We will sometimes recognize our own households in theirs.

And through those generations, we will hear God speak again of the promise.

Abraham would not live to see its whole course. Neither would Isaac. Jacob would receive words reaching far beyond what he could accomplish within his own lifetime.

Yet God continued to deal with them personally.

That brings us back to something we saw in the garden. The Creator cared about the relationship. Even after the man and woman hid, He came looking for them.

Now we are following His purpose into another household.

The people within it could tend their flocks, establish their homes, and make provision for their children. They could pass along possessions and teach what experience had taught them. But the deepest need of the human family remained beyond their power to repair.

They needed the One who had called them.

And He was binding His promise to the generations still to come.
