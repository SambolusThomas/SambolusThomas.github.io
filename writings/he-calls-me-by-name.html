He Calls Me by Name

When I was a little kid, my dad got me a transistor radio. You had to turn it on, turn it up, and turn the dial until you found the station you wanted. I could sit there with that little radio in my hand and hear nothing but static. Then I’d move the dial just a little, and there was somebody talking. That voice had been coming into the room the whole time.

I was thinking about that when I asked myself, How much attention am I giving to what God is saying? I can sit through a whole message at church and have my mind on something I need to do when I get home. Or I can listen and ask, Lord, is there something here You want me to hear today? Someone sitting next to me might come away thinking about a different part of the very same message.

Then I read what Jesus said about the shepherd in John 10:3–4. “He calleth his own sheep by name, and leadeth them out.” They follow him because they know his voice.

I have read that before. This time I stopped at by name.

When I think about God calling me by name, I think about all the conversations I’ve had with Him. I remember the times He held me up when I couldn’t stand spiritually. I remember the times I had to admit I couldn’t handle what was in front of me and call out to Him. I can look back on what He did in those moments, and even now it fills me with wonder.

Then I wonder what it would sound like to hear Him say, “Sam,” and have every one of those moments come back to me in an instant. Every time I called. Every time He held me up. All those years of knowing Him, coming back to me in the sound of my own name.

And before I hear Him say it, what is He thinking about? Does He think about those times anywhere close to the way I do? When I remember being held, does He remember holding me? I want to ask Him that. I know what those moments mean to me. I wonder what they mean to Him. The thought that He and I might remember those same moments together makes me reach for words I don’t have.

A little farther on, Jesus says, “My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me.” Then He says He gives them eternal life and that no one can pluck them out of His hand (John 10:27–28). I’m glad He put those things together. The One I am learning to listen to is the One who gave His life to bring me near. He knows me, and He holds me in His hand.

So I want to open His Word with that in mind. I can take my time over a verse I’ve read a hundred times and ask Him to show me what I’m passing over. Then I can carry it into the day and do what He has shown me. That’s part of learning to follow a voice I am coming to know.

He knows my name. I want to give Him my attention when He calls.
